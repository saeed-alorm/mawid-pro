MAWID Founder Brief

Status: Local draft only As of: 2026-08-24 (Asia/Beirut) Decision posture: Recommended — proceed to a narrow, structured Lebanon salon pilot to validate the problem, initial customer segment, and commercial model before pricing, hiring, or material investment.

Founder decision

MAWID should test the Missing question of whether a bilingual, private-workspace SaaS can help appointment-led salons and barbershops in Lebanon manage booking requests, staff schedules, and operational visibility more reliably. The founder decision at this stage is not to scale or claim product-market fit. It is to commission evidence that can either support, change, or stop this product thesis.

Product thesis

Missing: Whether appointment-led salons in the defined target segment value a connected booking, staff-schedule, and operational-visibility workflow enough to change behavior.

Confirmed: The MAWID prototype scope includes bilingual English/Arabic use, employee selection, pending-approval booking requests, calendar views, and management actions within a salon's private workspace.

This is a workflow thesis, not validated customer evidence. Customer pain, demand, willingness to pay, price tolerance, acquisition cost, churn, and addressable-market size remain Missing.

Authoritative boundary

Confirmed: MAWID is a fictional browser-local prototype of a bilingual, multi-tenant B2B appointment-booking and salon-management SaaS. A salon has a private workspace and a salon-branded customer booking experience. It is not a public marketplace.

The proposed business plan and pilot must preserve this boundary:

  • Private workspace per salon; no consumer discovery or cross-salon marketplace.
  • Bilingual English/Arabic, with Arabic RTL usability as a product requirement to be tested rather than a claim of adoption.
  • Connected customer booking, manager approval workflow, employee schedules, and salon operational views are the relevant product surface.
  • Local drafts and sanitized evidence only; no external-system actions or collection of unnecessary personal data.

Non-goals and exclusions

Confirmed: The initial plan excludes public marketplace behavior, real SMS/OTP, backend authentication, database integration, online payments or deposits, TV appointment displays, inventory, payroll, and POS.

Recommended: The pilot must also exclude hardware or digital signage, advertising, payment processing, POS, payroll, and custom per-salon applications. These exclusions protect the test from becoming an unbounded technology-services or hardware business.

Working hypotheses to test

  1. Missing: Target salons experience sufficiently material booking, scheduling, approval, or visibility problems for a dedicated workflow to be worth changing behavior for.
  2. Missing: A salon-branded customer booking flow with explicit employee selection and approval/rescheduling is preferable to the salon's current process for an identifiable early segment.
  3. Missing: Bilingual English/Arabic and RTL operation reduce friction for the relevant buyers, managers, staff, or customers.
  4. Missing: A narrow product containing booking, approvals, schedules, customer records, and operational views is enough to earn an initial commercial commitment without payments, POS, payroll, inventory, advertising, hardware, or bespoke applications.
  5. Missing: MAWID can acquire, onboard, retain, and support target salons within a viable 12-month operating model.

Immediate next decision sequence

Recommended: First define the initial interview segment and run the validation plan. Then synthesize sourced customer evidence before setting price, estimating demand, making market/revenue claims, or preparing an operating-capacity commitment. A pilot recommendation may be made; product-market fit must not be represented as Confirmed without the required evidence.
