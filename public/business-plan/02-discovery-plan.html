MAWID Discovery and Validation Plan — Lebanese Salons and Barbershops

Status: Recommended As of: 2026-08-24 (Asia/Beirut) Scope: Founder-led qualitative discovery only; no messages will be sent and no external system will be changed.

Evidence position

Confirmed: MAWID is a bilingual English/Arabic, private-workspace B2B appointment-booking and salon-management prototype. The documented customer flow includes a request that awaits salon approval, and the management flow includes approve, reject, and proposed-time actions. It is not a public marketplace and excludes payments, POS, payroll, inventory, and hardware in this version. Source: mawid-github/README.md.

Missing: No customer, salon, barbershop, owner, manager, employee, or end-customer interviews have occurred. Therefore every claim about customer pain, workflow prevalence, buying authority, willingness to pay, demand, language preference, WhatsApp usage, no-shows, cancellations, approval behavior, or data/privacy expectations is Missing. This plan must not be read as evidence that any of those claims are true.

Recommended: Run the interviews below before setting a price, selecting a primary segment, declaring market demand, hiring, or committing material pilot investment. The quotas are planning judgments for breadth of learning, not statistical proof of demand.

Founder operating procedure

Recommended: Use a founder or trained interviewer who is not pitching during the first 24 minutes. Start from the respondent's actual last booking, cancellation, no-show, approval, or reschedule rather than a generic opinion. Ask permission before taking notes; do not record audio unless the respondent explicitly consents. Do not collect customer names, phone numbers, message screenshots, payment information, or complete message threads.

Recommended: Aim for the respondent to speak at least 60% of the time. Ask no more than three situation questions before following their answer with a problem, implication, or need-payoff question. Treat a polite positive reaction as insufficient evidence; capture the example, frequency, consequence, current workaround, and decision owner.

30-minute guide: SPIN + Gap + Sandler

TimePurpose and methodFounder prompts
0–2 minUpfront contract (Sandler)“Thank you for the time. I’m learning how appointment-led salons operate; I’m not asking you to buy anything today. I’ll ask about your current process and a recent example. At the end, we can say whether it is useful to continue or not. Is that okay? May I take notes?”
2–5 minSituation; role and environment“What is your role in appointments?” “Please walk me through how a booking normally reaches you.” “Which people touch the process?” “Which language(s) do you and customers use?”
5–12 minCurrent-state map and recent event (SPIN Situation/Problem; Gap current state)“Think of the last booking you handled. What happened from first contact through the appointment?” “Where, if anywhere, does that process become difficult?” “Can you show the steps without showing client details?” “What have you tried?”
12–18 minRoot cause and impact (SPIN Implication; Sandler pain funnel; Gap impact)“When that happens, what happens next?” “How often does that specific situation occur in a typical week or month?” “What time, missed appointment, staff confusion, or customer experience effect follows?” “Who feels the consequence?” “Why has the current workaround remained?”
18–22 minDesired state and outcome (SPIN Need-payoff; Gap future state)“If this part worked reliably, what would be different in your day?” “What would you need to see change to call it worthwhile?” “What would still need to stay manual?”
22–25 minBuying process and onboarding“If you decided to change the process, who would decide, who would use it, and who could block it?” “What time or help could you realistically give to setup and staff training?” “What would make a trial too disruptive?”
25–28 minConcept reaction and price process; no leading claimBriefly show only the relevant documented workflow, if the respondent permits. Ask the non-leading questions below. Do not promise functionality, integrations, timing, or price.
28–30 minRecap and permissioned close“Let me check I understood: [respondent's words]. What did I miss?” “Is there someone else who sees this process differently?” “May I retain these de-identified notes for research?”

Concept boundary and non-leading pricing questions

Confirmed: The prototype supports a salon-branded customer booking experience, customer-selected employee or any available professional, pending salon approval, approve/reject/propose-time actions, employee scheduling, and bilingual EN/AR interface behavior. It does not support real SMS, payments, a backend, real authentication, POS, payroll, inventory, or a marketplace. Source: mawid-github/README.md.

Recommended: Only after current-state discovery, give a neutral description: “MAWID is a private salon workspace with a branded booking experience. A customer can request a slot, and the salon can approve, reject, or propose another time. It is a prototype and does not currently include real SMS, payments, POS, payroll, inventory, or a public marketplace.” Then ask:

  1. “What, if anything, in that description relates to the process you described?”
  2. “What would make it not useful or not usable for you?”
  3. “What would have to be true before you would consider trying a system like that?”
  4. “How do you currently decide whether a business tool is worth paying for?”
  5. “What costs or trade-offs would you compare against: time, staff effort, an existing tool, missed appointments, something else, or none?”
  6. “What range, if any, would make you stop considering a monthly tool before seeing a trial? What makes that range feel too high or reasonable?”
  7. “Would you expect a one-time setup/training charge, a monthly charge, a per-location charge, another basis, or none? Why?”
  8. “Who would approve a spend like this, and what would they need to see?”
  9. “If a trial were offered, what evidence would you require before continuing it?”

Missing: A stated price range, positive concept reaction, or intent to try is not a purchase. Record it as a respondent-specific statement, not proof of market demand or a validated price.

Evidence coding and quality scheme

Recommended: Code individual excerpts and observations, not the interviewer’s overall impression. A theme becomes a candidate only after the underlying excerpts are traceable to respondent IDs and segments.

FieldAllowed values / code
Evidence labelConfirmed = directly observed in the specified source; Inferred = bounded interpretation from recorded evidence; Conflicting = materially different respondent evidence; Missing = no adequate evidence; Recommended = proposed next action or test
Evidence typeQUOTE, OBSERVED_WORKFLOW, REPORTED_EVENT, CONCEPT_REACTION, BUYING_PROCESS, CONSTRAINT
ThemeINTAKE, WHATSAPP_MANUAL, LANGUAGE_RTL, AVAILABILITY, EMPLOYEE_SELECTION, APPROVAL, CANCELLATION_NO_SHOW, RESCHEDULE, ONBOARDING, BUYING_AUTHORITY, PRICE_PROCESS, DATA_PRIVACY, NON_FIT
SpecificityS1 opinion only; S2 concrete example; S3 concrete example plus frequency/consequence or observed workflow
DirectionSUPPORTS, CHALLENGES, CONDITIONAL, NO_SIGNAL
Confidence for synthesisC1 single, low-specificity response; C2 one concrete response or repeated low-specificity signal; C3 at least two concrete responses in distinct establishments with no material conflict; C4 repeated concrete/observed evidence across at least two target segments, with disconfirming evidence checked

Recommended: Before coding a theme as C3 or C4, actively look for a counterexample: a respondent who uses a different process, reports no problem, solves it acceptably, lacks authority, or considers the workflow irrelevant. Preserve that evidence rather than averaging it away.

Synthesis method

  1. Recommended: Within 24 hours, de-identify and normalize each note. Split it into atomic evidence items with respondent ID, segment, theme, evidence type, quote/observation, specificity, direction, and evidence label.
  2. Recommended: Create a respondent-by-theme matrix. Keep role, segment, current tool/process, and decision role visible so frequency is not confused with independence or buying authority.
  3. Recommended: For each theme, separate current-state evidence, desired-state evidence, buying-process evidence, constraints, and concept reactions. Do not merge them into a single positive/negative score.
  4. Recommended: Write a one-sentence finding only where the source excerpts support it. Use Confirmed only for the direct material; mark cross-interview interpretation Inferred. If accounts materially differ, use Conflicting.
  5. Recommended: Produce a disconfirming-evidence column, a segment comparison, and an evidence-gap list. Distinguish a recommendation for a pilot test from a conclusion of demand.
  6. Recommended: Give the Feedback Synthesizer the coded matrix, de-identified notes, theme count by distinct establishment, contradictions, and a decision memo. Do not provide raw personal information or infer willingness to pay from politeness.

Research safeguards

Recommended: Keep all notes local and de-identified. Use a respondent ID in synthesis. Do not upload, send, or paste data into external tools. Do not retain client lists, phone numbers, exact addresses, payment information, complete message threads, photos/screenshots, or employee information that is not needed for the research question. If a respondent asks about privacy or legal obligations, record the question as a constraint and route it to the legal/compliance workstream; do not provide legal assurance.

Recommended: The founder should openly state that MAWID is a prototype, distinguish described capability from a promise, and end a conversation without a sales push. A request for a demo, pilot, or follow-up is evidence of interest only until an explicit, permissioned next step occurs.
