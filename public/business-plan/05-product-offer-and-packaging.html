MAWID Product Offer and Packaging — Evidence-Gated Pilot Brief

Status: Local draft only As of: 2026-08-25 (Asia/Beirut) Decision use: Define a discovery-oriented pilot concept; not a validated ICP, price, roadmap commitment, commercial launch, or production-readiness decision.

Product decision

Confirmed: MAWID is specified as a browser-local, bilingual English/Arabic, multi-tenant B2B appointment-booking and salon-management prototype. Each salon operates a private workspace and salon-branded booking experience; MAWID is not a public marketplace. Source: mawid-github/README.md.

Missing: There is no direct customer evidence that any salon or barbershop needs, prefers, will adopt, or will pay for the described workflow. There is no validated primary ICP, buyer, price, onboarding model, or operating outcome.

Recommended: Run a narrow, discovery-oriented pilot concept around one connected private-workspace workflow: salon setup → service/professional selection → appointment request → manager decision or alternative-time proposal → employee calendar and customer-status update. Treat every stated benefit as a hypothesis to test, not a product promise.

Recommended pilot value proposition hypothesis

Recommended: “MAWID may give an appointment-led salon or barbershop a private, salon-branded bilingual booking workspace for collecting booking requests, coordinating staff availability, and managing approval, rejection, rescheduling, and customer status—without becoming a public marketplace or adding transaction or back-office systems.”

Missing: The problem severity, language relevance, workflow fit, buyer value, and commercial value of this proposition are unproven. The hypothesis must be tested against a recent, concrete current-state workflow before showing a concept or asking commercial questions.

Packaging hypotheses — no MAWID price decision

Confirmed: Official current public pages show that Fresha, Booksy, and SimplyBook.me use different public packaging bases, including a per-bookable-team-member component, a base plan plus additional users, and plan limits based on bookings/features/providers. These are locale-specific benchmarks, not Lebanese willingness-to-pay evidence. Sources: 03-market-sizing-and-competition.md; Fresha, Booksy, and SimplyBook.me official pricing pages cited there.

A staff- or bookable-professional dimension may be useful only if staffing structure affects workflow value or support burden.RecommendedCompetitor public packaging shows this as one possible pricing basis; MAWID has no local evidence for it.That team size predicts willingness to pay or that a per-person fee is appropriate.

Pilot access should be governed by a defined evaluation scope and evidence plan, not an implied free-to-paid conversion.RecommendedPrevents the pilot from substituting speculative conversion for learning.That trial length, conversion, or a paid pilot is validated.

Missing: MAWID price points, billing currency, taxes, discounts, trial duration, setup fees, contract term, plan limits, conversion rate, acquisition cost, support cost, and retention are unsupported. Finance may model them only as editable scenario assumptions with a source-and-assumption label.

Prioritization logic

Recommended: Prioritize discovery work that can falsify the narrow product thesis quickly: first verify a recent appointment workflow and role ownership; then assess whether the private booking/approval/schedule concept fits; then assess setup, exclusions, language, and commercial process. This sequence reduces the risk of building or pricing against an unverified problem.

Recommended: Defer all expansion requests—including marketplace reach, payments, POS, payroll, advertising, hardware, inventory, and bespoke apps—because accepting them would make a scope-fit test unable to distinguish core workflow value from an unbounded services or commerce offering.
